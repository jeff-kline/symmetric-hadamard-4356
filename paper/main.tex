\begin{abstract}
This note updates two entries of the 2025 census of Djokovi\'c and Seberry, which lists $v=529$ and $v=1089$ among the odd $v<2500$ for which the existence of a symmetric Hadamard matrix of order $4v$ is undecided. No new construction is involved. A theorem of Wallis and Whiteman (1972) gives a regular symmetric Hadamard matrix with constant diagonal of order $(p-1)^2$ whenever $p$ is a prime power and a symmetric conference matrix of order $p-1$ exists. Mathon's conference matrix of order $46$ (1978) with $p=47$, and Gritsenko's of order $66$ (2021) with $p=67$, give the orders $46^2=2116=4\cdot529$ and $66^2=4356=4\cdot1089$. SageMath implements this construction and recognizes both orders, and a public index has since recorded 2116 by another route. We record the construction, with an exact computer check at order 4356. Both ingredients are published; the only contribution is the observation that together they settle these two entries.
\end{abstract}

\section{The census entries}
The census~\cite{DS} lists, for odd $v<2500$, the orders $4v$ at which a symmetric Hadamard matrix is known, series by series, and the 595 values of $v$ for which existence is undecided. The values $v=529=23^2$ and $v=1089=33^2$ are on that list. None of its named series is the Wallis--Whiteman construction used below. SageMath~\cite{SAGE} implements that construction~\cite[Theorem~16, Corollary~17]{WW}, includes Mathon's graph on 45 vertices and, since 2021, Gritsenko's graph on 65 vertices, and recognizes both orders $2116$ and $4356$. Djokovi\'c's later paper~\cite{D25} settles three further values, not including $1089$. The public index~\cite{GSI} (checked 19 November 2026) records a symmetric Hadamard matrix of order $2116$, by a propus construction, and a skew Hadamard matrix of order $4356$ but no symmetric one. Goryainov, Haemers, Konstantinova and Li~\cite{GHKL} list the Wallis--Whiteman construction among the known constructions of regular symmetric Hadamard matrices with constant diagonal and, separately, mention Gritsenko's graph on $65$ vertices, which gives a conference matrix of order $66$; they do not draw the consequence for order $4356$.

\section{The construction}
Let $G$ be an abelian group of order $2m+1$. Two $m$-subsets $X,Y\subset G$ are \emph{Szekeres difference sets} if every nonzero element of $G$ arises exactly $m-1$ times as a difference of two elements of $X$ or of two elements of $Y$. The type~1 incidence matrix of $S\subset G$ is $M_S=(\epsilon_{g,h})$, with $\epsilon_{g,h}=1$ if $h-g\in S$ and $-1$ otherwise. A symmetric conference matrix of order $4m+2$ can be normalized to $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&f\\ f^{\mathsf T}&W\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $f$ is a row of ones; then $W=W^{\mathsf T}$, $W$ has zero diagonal, $WJ=0$ and $W^2=(4m+1)I-J$.

\begin{theorem}[Wallis--Whiteman~{\cite[Theorem~16]{WW}}]
Let $X,Y$ be Szekeres difference sets of size $m$ in an abelian group of order $2m+1$, with $X\cap(-X)=\emptyset$ and $Y=-Y$, and suppose a symmetric conference matrix of order $4m+2$ exists. Put $B=M_X$, $A=-M_Y$, let $e$ and $f$ be rows of ones of lengths $2m+1$ and $4m+1$, let $\otimes$ be the Kronecker product, and put
\[
P_\pm=\pm A\otimes W+J\otimes I,\qquad R=-(B+I)\otimes W+I\otimes J+(I-J)\otimes I.
\]
Then
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}
1 & f & e\otimes f & -e\otimes f\\
f^{\mathsf T} & J & e\otimes(W-I) & e\otimes(W+I)\\
e^{\mathsf T}\otimes f^{\mathsf T} & e^{\mathsf T}\otimes(W-I) & P_+ & R\\
-e^{\mathsf T}\otimes f^{\mathsf T} & e^{\mathsf T}\otimes(W+I) & R^{\mathsf T} & P_-
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a regular symmetric Hadamard matrix of order $4(2m+1)^2$ with constant diagonal.
\end{theorem}

After Theorem~16, Wallis and Whiteman note, citing Szekeres, that such sets exist for $m=(p-3)/4$, $p$ a prime power. For order $2116$ one takes $m=11$, $p=47$ and Mathon's conference matrix of order $46$~\cite{M78}; SageMath implements this case, and we do not repeat it. For order $4356$ we use $m=16$, $p=67$ and the following explicit sets, which the program in Section~3 verifies. Let $Q$ be the group of nonzero squares in $\F_{67}$, cyclic of order $33$, and take
\[
X=\{x\in Q: x-1\in Q\},\qquad Y=\{y\in Q: y+1\in Q\},
\]
written additively through a discrete logarithm. Since $-1$ is a nonsquare, $x\in X$ implies $x^{-1}\notin X$, while $Y$ is closed under inversion.

For $W$ take the Seidel matrix of Gritsenko's graph~\cite{G21}: $W=J-I-2\,\mathrm{Adj}$, of order $65$. Its strong regularity with parameters $(65,32,15,16)$ gives $W^2=65I-J$ and $WJ=0$, so bordering $W$ by a row and column of ones gives a symmetric conference matrix of order $66$.

\section{Verification}
A short program checks every step in exact arithmetic. The printed adjacency matrix of~\cite{G21} is symmetric with zero diagonal and row sums $32$, and adjacent and nonadjacent pairs have $15$ and $16$ common neighbours; the bordered matrix $C$ of order $66$ satisfies $C^2=65I$. The sets $X,Y$ have size $16$, and their differences cover every nonzero element of $\mathbb Z_{33}$ exactly $15$ times. The relations $B^{\mathsf T}+B=-2I$, $A=A^{\mathsf T}$, $AB=BA$, $AJ=J$, $BJ=-J$ and $AA^{\mathsf T}+BB^{\mathsf T}=68I-2J$ hold. The assembled $H$ has order $4356$, entries $\pm1$, $H=H^{\mathsf T}$, diagonal entries $1$, every row and column sum $66$, and $HH^{\mathsf T}=4356\,I$, computed in full. The run takes a few seconds on one processor core. A second program, sharing no code with the first, re-checks every pair of rows.

\section{Remarks}
The same theorem applies whenever $a\equiv2\pmod4$, $a+1$ is a prime power and a symmetric conference matrix of order $a$ exists; the order is $a^2=4(a/2)^2$. For $2<a<100$ this gives $v=a^2/4\in\{9,25,81,169,225,441,529,1089,1681\}$ from $a=6,10,18,26,30,42,46,66,82$. The census decides all of these except $529$ and $1089$, so the theorem settles exactly these two census entries below $2500$. Both orders follow from published ingredients and are recognized by SageMath; the public index~\cite{GSI} records $2116$ by another route. We identified $4356$ independently, without consulting SageMath. A later survey of SageMath's constructors against the census showed that it already recognizes $4356$, and also $2116$. Apart from these, we found no earlier record of either order in the sources examined; this is not a claim of global priority.
